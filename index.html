About

I’m a mechanical engineer who builds aircraft that fly without a pilot on board. From 2016 to 2024 I taught and did research at the Department of Mechanical Engineering, Universitas Riau, in Pekanbaru, where my group designed, built and flew the Serindit V-1 and V-2 UAVs and the Atha Mapper 2150.

My work sits where aerodynamics meets hardware. CFD and experimental propeller tests feed straight into airframes we then build and fly, along with the parts around them: high-lift devices, bungee-cord launchers, landing parachutes and frames that let a fixed-wing take off vertically. I also care about local, low-cost materials, including fuselages made from oil palm empty fruit bunch fiber.

Since April 2024 I’ve been a doctoral student in the Department of Mechanical Systems Engineering at Tokyo Metropolitan University, Japan, working on bio-inspired flapping-wing aerial robots.

Research

Six threads run through my work. Pick one to see the papers behind it.

Fixed-wing UAVs

Serindit V-1, Serindit V-2 and Atha Mapper 2150: wing and high-lift design, propeller matching, bungee launch and parachute recovery.

VTOL and tricopter hybrids

Fixed-wing VTOL layouts, tail-sitter concepts and a tricopter frame that turns a fixed-wing airframe into a vertical take-off platform.

Flapping-wing robots

My doctoral research at Tokyo Metropolitan University: aerial robots that fly by flapping, the way birds and insects do.

Aerodynamics and CFD

Ansys Fluent simulations and bench tests that tell us how a design will fly before we risk the airframe.

Natural-fiber airframes

Oil palm empty fruit bunch composites as a cheap, local material for UAV fuselages, plus earlier work on non-asbestos brake materials.

Regenerative suspension

My master’s work: a hydraulic-motor shock absorber that recovers energy from vehicle vibration, and how it changes ride response.
